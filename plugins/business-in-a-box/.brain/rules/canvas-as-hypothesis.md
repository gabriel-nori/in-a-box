THE RULE

A business model canvas describes one business model on one page, and each block is a claim to test, not a fact.

- Nine blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, cost structure.
- Scope it to one model. Different segments with different models get different canvases.
- The blocks depend on each other: changing the segment changes channels, activities and costs. Re-read the whole canvas after changing one block.
- Mark what is known and what is a hypothesis, and date the canvas so it can be redrawn as evidence arrives.

WHY

Trigger: someone asks to model a business, map how it earns money, or design a new offer.

Strategyzer describes the canvas as a one-page description of how a business creates, delivers and captures value, "a claim, not a plan". Its page does not list the nine blocks; the list above is the skill's own and the standard Osterwalder list.

HOW TO APPLY

Fill the blocks from what the person gave; mark every inference as a hypothesis; name which hypothesis is riskiest and how to test it.
