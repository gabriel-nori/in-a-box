# The Scrum Sprint, in the order its events happen

## Purpose
Run one Sprint so that it produces a usable Increment and ends with a plan to improve. The events are in a process because the Scrum Guide fixes their order: planning opens the Sprint, the Daily Scrum runs inside it, the review inspects the Increment, and the retrospective closes it before the next planning.

## Trigger
The start of a Sprint, which begins right after the previous one ends. A Sprint is a fixed length of one month or less.

## Participants
The Scrum Team: Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers. Stakeholders join the review. Others may be invited to planning for advice.

## Inputs
- The ordered Product Backlog and the Product Owner's proposal of how it adds value this Sprint.
- The Developers' past performance and capacity, and the Definition of Done.

## Outcomes
- Sprint planning yields a Sprint Goal and a Sprint Backlog (the goal, the selected items and a plan). Timebox: at most 8 hours for a one-month Sprint.
- The Daily Scrum yields an actionable plan for the next day and surfaced impediments, every working day, 15 minutes, same time and place.
- The Sprint Review yields a possibly adjusted Product Backlog and agreed next steps. Timebox: at most 4 hours for a one-month Sprint.
- The Retrospective yields the most helpful improvements, which may enter the next Sprint Backlog. It comes after the review and before the next planning. Timebox: at most 3 hours for a one-month Sprint.

## How it is handled
- Planning comes first. The Product Owner proposes how the product could increase its value in the Sprint; the team collaborates on the Sprint Goal; the Developers select the items and plan the work themselves; the plan is visible and updated through the Sprint.
- The Daily Scrum comes next, every day. The Developers pick the structure; the point is progress toward the goal, not a status report. Longer discussions are taken straight after, with the people concerned. The Scrum Master helps keep it inside 15 minutes and removes impediments.
- The Review comes near the end. The team shows what was done and what was not, and what changed in the environment. Stakeholders and the team work on what to do next; it is a working session. The Product Owner adjusts the backlog from what is learned.
- The Retrospective closes the Sprint. The team inspects how it went and what would help most. Chosen improvements become work with an owner. The record says what blocked a thing, never who failed.

## Expectations of the position
- **Product Owner:** brings the ordered backlog and the goal-level why; does not assign work; receives review feedback and decides what enters the backlog.
- **Developers:** own the plan, the forecast, the Daily Scrum and the honest presentation of the Increment, including what is not done.
- **Scrum Master:** makes sure each event happens, is positive and productive, and stays in its timebox; does not run the Daily Scrum as a reporting meeting.
- **Stakeholders:** give feedback; they change the backlog by persuading the Product Owner.
