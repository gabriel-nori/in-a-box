THE RULE

A pitch is short, ordered from problem to ask, and states every claim it can support.

- Kawasaki's 10/20/30 is a heuristic: about ten slides, twenty minutes, nothing smaller than 30-point text. It is one author's advice, not a standard; adapt it to the audience.
- The order runs from the problem the audience feels, through the solution and why it wins, to what is asked of them.
- Numbers and market claims carry a source or are marked `assumption`.
- Match the pitch to who listens: an investor, a customer and a partner need different proofs.

WHY

Trigger: someone asks for a pitch, a deck outline, or how to present an idea.

Kawasaki's own page returned 403, so the rule rests on secondary pages repeating it. Sequoia's template was not found at the source and is not cited.

HOW TO APPLY

Name the audience and the ask first; build the story backward from the ask; cut anything the ask does not need.
