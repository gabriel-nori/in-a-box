THE RULE

A SWOT separates inside from outside, and every item is traceable.

- Strengths and weaknesses are internal to the subject; opportunities and threats are external. A competitor's strength is a threat, never the subject's weakness.
- Each item names its evidence or is marked `assumption`.
- The value is in pairing: use a strength against an opportunity, protect against a threat that hits a weakness. A SWOT with no actions is a list.
- SWOT is subjective and goes stale: record who judged it and when, and refresh it when the environment changes.

WHY

Trigger: someone asks for a SWOT, a strengths-and-weaknesses view, or a quick assessment of an idea or company.

The guides found agree on the 2x2 split, pairing factors into strategies, subjectivity, and refreshing. No single authoritative source exists for SWOT, so this rule is the common ground of secondary guides, not a standard.

HOW TO APPLY

Scope first, then the four quadrants, then at least the strongest strength-to-opportunity match and the most dangerous weakness-to-threat match with one action each. List unknowns instead of filling them.
